The rule behind every row: VRAM ≈ weights on disk × 1.6 for video at 480p, and VRAM ≈ weights + 1.5 GB for images at 1 MP. Q4_K_M is the last stop before quality falls off a cliff.

03 — Tokens

Your prompt has a budget.

Three ways to stop paying for text

Delete the negative prompt at CFG 1.0 — it is mathematically ignored, so encoding it is pure waste. Batch several clips from one prompt so one encode serves all of them. And move the encoder onto the GPU: that is the real difference between an 8 GB card and a 12 GB one.

01

GPU & VRAM

The card holds the diffusion model and the activations for the current frame. Everything else — the encoder, the OS, the browser — competes for the same 6 GB.

Free VRAM beats a faster GPU. Closing a browser can be worth more than a tier upgrade.

02

The diffusion model

Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B is a 5-billion-parameter transformer that denoises a video latent. It is the only component that must sit fully on the GPU to be fast.

At 3.3 GiB quantised it leaves ~1.5 GB for activations. At 5.4 GiB (Q8) it does not, which is why Q8 is not an option on 6 GB.

03

GGUF quantisation

Weights are compressed from 16-bit to 4-bit blocks (Q4_K_M). Quality loss is small at Q4 and grows quickly below Q3. GGUF is the format; ComfyUI-GGUF is the loader.

The whole trick. 10 GB fp16 → 3.3 GiB Q4_K_M, with the model still recognisably the same model.

04

Text encoder

umt5-xxl turns your prompt into conditioning vectors. It is enormous: 4.66 GB dequantised, more than the video model.

It does not fit next to the UNet, so it streams from system RAM and costs 60–125 s per prompt. This is the real low-end tax.

05

Conditioning & CFG

Classifier-free guidance mixes a positive and a negative prediction. CFG 1.0 means “ignore the negative entirely”, which is what Turbo models are distilled for.

CFG 1.0 halves the model passes and makes the negative prompt free to delete. Keep CFG 6 for VACE; it is not distilled.

06

The latent

Video is compressed 8× spatially and 4× temporally before sampling. 81 frames become 21 latent frames of 48 channels.

This is why frame counts are 4n+1 and why 81 frames cost far less than 81× a single image.

07

Sampler

Steps, sampler and scheduler decide how the noise is walked down. Turbo needs 4 steps with euler + simple; more steps buy polish, not structure.

Time is linear in steps. Four is the floor; six is the only upgrade worth paying for.

08

Sampling shift

ModelSamplingSD3 with shift 8.0 biases sampling toward low noise. It is part of the Turbo recipe, not a quality slider.

Wrong shift looks like “washed out” or “burned contrast”. Leave it at 8.0 unless you are deliberately experimenting.

09

VAE

The VAE decodes the latent back to pixels. Wan 2.2 and Wan 2.1 have different VAEs and they are not interchangeable.

Wrong VAE is the #1 cause of black, grey or discoloured video. 2.2 graph → 2.2 VAE; VACE graph → 2.1 VAE.

10

Resolution

Cost is roughly quadratic in pixels, and activations are what overflow. 704×480 is the model's trained size and the largest that fits in 6 GB.

832×480 is +18% pixels but 1.8× the time. It crosses the VRAM line and starts swapping. Do not.

11

Frames & fps

81 frames at 24 fps is 3.4 s. The same frames at 16 fps is a slower, more cinematic 5 s. Frames cost time; fps costs nothing.

Reduce frames before resolution when you are tight on memory — frames scale linearly, resolution does not.

12

Upscaler

4× UltraSharp is a small ESRGAN that sharpens and enlarges decoded frames. It runs after sampling, so it does not touch the model's VRAM budget.

The 1080p path: render 864×480, upscale 4×, resize to 1920×1080. Real pixels, interpolated detail.

06 — The budget

Where 6 GB actually goes.

Two different memory pools, two different failure modes. The GPU runs out of VRAM; the encoder runs out of patience in system RAM.

VRAM · 6.0 GB

peak 5.7 GB
  • UNet Q4_K_M, resident3.4 GB
  • attention + activations1.5 GB
  • VAE decode spike0.6 GB
  • desktop + driver0.3 GB

Latents are negligible: 81 frames → 21 × 60 × 88 × 48 values ≈ 10 MB.

System RAM · 16 GB

encoder streams
  • umt5-xxl dequantised4.7 GB
  • ComfyUI + Python + torch3.5 GB
  • Windows, browser, pagefilerest

This is why 16 GB is the practical floor and 32 GB makes the run visibly smoother.

The offload, in one sentence

The UNet gets the GPU because it is touched every step; the encoder gets system RAM because it is touched once per prompt. Swapping that assignment is the difference between 4.5 and 24.7 seconds per step.

4.5 sper step, UNet resident
24.7 sper step, --lowvram
5.5×the same job, wrong flag

09 — Install

From empty folder to first clip.

Windows portable. Linux and macOS are in the docs, same five steps with a venv.

  1. 1 ComfyUI portable

    Download the .7z from the ComfyUI releases page and extract it so that ComfyUI_windows_portable\python_embeded\python.exe exists.

    ComfyUI releases ↗

  2. 2 GGUF loader

    ComfyUI cannot read .gguf without this node.

    git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/city96/ComfyUI-GGUF.git `
  .\ComfyUI_windows_portable\ComfyUI\custom_nodes\ComfyUI-GGUF
.\ComfyUI_windows_portable\python_embeded\python.exe -m pip install `
  -r .\ComfyUI_windows_portable\ComfyUI\custom_nodes\ComfyUI-GGUF\requirements.txt

  3. 3 Models

    Six files, ~9 GB, resumable. The script also converts the upscaler to safetensors.

    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\scripts\download_models.ps1

  4. 4 Workflows

    Copy the four graphs into the sidebar folder.

    Copy-Item .\workflows\*.json `
  .\ComfyUI_windows_portable\ComfyUI\user\default\workflows\

  5. 5 Run

    Open http://127.0.0.1:8188, pick a workflow, press Run.

    .\scripts\run_comfyui.bat
Do not click “Install missing models”. The template browser points at full-precision safetensors — 10 GB for the 5B model, 28–60 GB for the 14B ones. On 6 GB they will never load. Use the GGUF graphs instead.

10 — When it breaks

The eight failures you will actually hit.

CUDA out of memory

Close GPU apps first (browser, Discord, Steam). Then drop resolution 704×480 → 640×384 → 512×288, then frames 81 → 49 → 33. Only then try --lowvram, which is 2.4× slower but peaks at 3.8 GB.

A model is missing from the dropdown

ComfyUI scans model folders once at startup — restart it. If it is still missing, the file is in the wrong subfolder: .gguf goes in models/diffusion_models, not checkpoints.

The template wants 50 GB of models

Ignore it. The templates reference fp16 checkpoints. Close the template browser and open one of the four GGUF graphs instead.

Black, grey or discoloured video

VAE mismatch. Wan 2.2 graphs need wan2.2_vae.safetensors; the VACE graph needs wan_2.1_vae.safetensors. They are not interchangeable.

It sits there for two minutes before sampling

That is the encoder pass. 4.66 GB does not fit next to the UNet, so it streams from RAM. A Q3_K_M encoder cuts it by ~40%; --highvram only OOMs.

1080p graph dies at the upscale

Select UpscaleModelLoader and press Ctrl+B to bypass it. The graph falls back to a direct Lanczos resize — softer, but it never fails.

VACE ignores the reference photo

Use a clear, single-subject portrait. The image must be in input slot 5 (reference_image). Lower strength to 0.8–0.9 if the pose is frozen.

Face swap finds no face

YuNet needs both eyes and the mouth. Frontal, well-lit, at least ~150 px wide. Check last_src_crop.png next to the output to see what the aligner saw.

11 — Questions

Short answers, no hedging.

Can I sample natively at 1080p?

No. 1920×1080 needs roughly 20 GB of VRAM for this model. The 1080p workflow renders at 864×480 and upscales; the file is 1080p, the detail is not.

Why 704×480 and not 720p?

480p is the trained resolution. 720p is 2.25× the pixels and pushes activations past 6 GB, which turns a 4.5 s step into a 35–40 s step.

Why 4 steps?

Turbo is distilled for 4 steps at CFG 1.0. More steps add polish at linear cost; they do not fix composition. VACE is a different, non-distilled model and needs 20 steps at CFG 6.

Can the text encoder go on the GPU?

On 6 GB, only if you free ~1.5 GB of VRAM or switch to the Q3_K_M encoder (~2.7 GiB). The Q4 encoder never fits next to the UNet.

Does this work on AMD or Apple?

AMD via ComfyUI-Zluda (Windows) or ROCm (Linux); Apple via MPS, roughly 5–10× slower. Only NVIDIA was measured here. The GGUF files are the same.

What is the difference between VACE and the face-swap script?

VACE generates a new clip from a photo and a prompt — motion is invented. The script edits an existing video frame by frame — motion is preserved exactly. Neither is a reliable identity lock at 1.3B / 128 px.

Is the 1080p workflow tested?

No, and it is labelled experimental on purpose. The graph is sound (480p sample → 4× upscale → resize) but the end-to-end run was not benchmarked on the reference machine.

Can I use this commercially?

Wan weights are Apache-2.0 and ComfyUI is GPL-3.0. Check the upscaler licence, and note inswapper_128 is research-only. Likeness rights are a separate legal question.
